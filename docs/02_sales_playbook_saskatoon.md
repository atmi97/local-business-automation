## 5. The Staff Training Script: The "7-Word Ask"

The biggest reason review stands fail is staff forgetting to mention them. Give the owner this exact cheat-sheet for their team:

> **The Rule**: Never ask *"Can you leave us a review?"* (People say no or feel pressured).
> 
> **The 7-Word Ask**: 
> While handing the receipt or terminal:
> **"Did you love everything about today's visit?"**
> 
> When the customer smiles and says **"Yes, it was amazing!"**, the staff immediately follows with:
> **"Awesome! Tap your phone right here to let our manager know."**

This converts over 70% of happy customers because it frames the action as appreciating the employee rather than doing work for the business.

## 6. Complete Objection Handling Matrix

### Objection 1: "We already have a QR code on our receipt / counter."
- **The Psychology**: The owner thinks QR codes and NFC are the same thing.
- **Battle-Tested Response**: 
  > *"QR codes are helpful, but have you noticed how few people actually pull out their camera, frame it, and wait for the link? In Canada, 80%+ of phone users pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay. They are already trained to tap. NFC has zero friction—they don't even open an app. Plus, our smart stands have both NFC and a backup dynamic QR code. Let’s put this stand next to your paper QR code for 7 days and see which one gets 5x more reviews."*

### Objection 2: "What if a dissatisfied customer taps it and leaves a 1-star review?"
- **The Psychology**: Fear of public embarrassment or losing their rating.
- **Battle-Tested Response**: 
  > *"I’m so glad you brought that up—that’s actually the #1 reason business owners switch to our system. With a basic Google link, an upset customer goes straight to public Google Maps. With our smart system, if someone taps and wants to leave 1, 2, or 3 stars, it routes them directly to a private manager message form so you can resolve the issue privately before it ever touches your public Google rating. It acts like a shield for your business."*

### Objection 3: "Google doesn't allow incentivizing reviews / Is this legal?"
- **The Psychology**: Fear of getting their Google Business Profile suspended.
- **Battle-Tested Response**: 
  > *"100% correct, and you should never pay customers or offer free food for reviews. That violates Google’s terms. What our card does is 100% compliant: it simply removes the technical friction for genuine, paying customers to find your public review page. Google actively encourages businesses to ask for reviews at the point of sale."*

### Objection 4: "I need to talk to my business partner / I don't have time right now."
- **The Psychology**: Brush-off or fear of making a decision.
- **Battle-Tested Response**: 
  > *"Totally respect that. I know you're running a busy shop. Tell you what: I'll leave this stand active on your counter right now. Don't pay me anything today. Show your partner tonight, let your customers tap it this weekend. I'll drop by Tuesday at 2 PM. If your partner hates the new 5-star reviews you got, I'll take it back. Does that work for you?"*

### Objection 5: "We already have a 4.8-star rating, we're doing fine."
- **The Psychology**: Complacency.
- **Battle-Tested Response**: 
  > *"You guys have an incredible rating, and congratulations on that! But here’s the secret Google doesn't advertise: Google Maps doesn't just rank based on star average; it ranks heavily on 'Review Velocity' and 'Recency'. If your competitor gets 20 reviews this month and you got 2, Google starts pushing their pin above yours in Saskatoon search results. Our system ensures you never fall behind."*
