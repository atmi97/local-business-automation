## 1. Tier 1: High-Ticket / High-Trust Behavioral Playbook

### Psychological Mindset
Cosmetic dentists, medspa directors, and specialty auto contractors do not think in terms of "getting 50 quick reviews." They think in terms of **prestige, trust, and risk mitigation**. 
- A single cosmetic patient or commercial roofing job is worth thousands of dollars.
- A single public 1-star review claiming malpractice or poor customer care can cost them $50,000+ in prospective patient cancellations.

### Step 1: Navigating the Receptionist Gatekeeper (The "Practice Coordinator Protocol")
Never walk into a medical clinic or dental office and say: *"Is the owner in today?"* (You will be instantly screened as a salesperson and told: *"We don't accept solicitations"*).

**Word-for-Word Script**:
```text
You: "Good morning! Quick question for your practice manager or clinical coordinator — who oversees your patient experience and Google Business Profile relations here in Saskatoon?"
Receptionist: "That would be Sarah, our office manager. What is this regarding?"
You: "I run a local patient reputation agency here in Saskatoon. We just deployed the direct contactless feedback system for practices on 8th Street, and I wanted to drop off our 30-day compliance overview for Dr. [Name]. Is Sarah available for literally 60 seconds, or is she with a patient?"
```

### Step 2: The High-Ticket Consultative Pitch
When speaking with the Practice Owner or Office Manager:
```text
"Hi Sarah, thank you for 60 seconds. I'll get straight to the point.
In high-end aesthetics/dental here in Saskatoon, prospective patients research your Google rating before they ever pick up the phone. A 4.3 or 4.4 star rating sends high-paying patients directly to your competitors down 8th Street.
More importantly: right now, if a patient has a minor misunderstanding about billing or wait times, they go straight home and post an emotional 1-star review on public Google.
Our system provides a dual benefit:
1. Contactless VIP review stands at your checkout console that make it effortless for your happiest patients to leave 5 stars before walking out.
2. An automatic Private Resolution Shield that intercepts patients who feel their experience was less than 4 stars, routing them directly to your personal email so you resolve it privately before it ever touches your public Google profile.
We’re placing this on a 14-day zero-risk practice pilot for premier clinics in Saskatoon. Can I set one up on your reception desk today?"
```

### Key Objections in Tier 1:
- **"We need to follow privacy/HIPAA/Canadian health guidelines."**
  - *Response*: *"100% agreed. Our system collects zero patient medical data. It simply provides a physical contactless NFC touchpoint that redirects them to public Google or a private feedback form. It is 100% compliant with Canadian privacy and Google Business standards."*

## 2. Tier 2: High Foot-Traffic / Impulse Reviews Behavioral Playbook

### Psychological Mindset
Restaurant owners, barbers, and cafe managers are operational grinders. They work 12-hour days, worry about food costs, employee turnover, and customer volume. They don't have time for a 30-minute slide deck. They need to see that it works in **3 seconds**.

### Step 1: The Timing Rule (Never Violate This)
- **Restaurants & Cafes**: Arrive at 10:15 AM (after prep, before 11:30 AM lunch rush) or 2:15 PM (after lunch plates are cleared). If you walk in at 12:15 PM, you will ruin your chances forever.
- **Barbershops**: Wednesday or Thursday morning when chairs are not 100% full.

### Step 2: The 3-Second Pattern Interrupt
Walk in with the hardware visible in your hand (never in a bag):
```text
You: "Hey! Are you the owner or general manager here?"
(Owner steps up):
You: "Awesome. Quick question — do you have your phone in your pocket? Pull it out really quickly. Don't open an app, just wake the screen."
(Hold your card against the top of their phone. Their own Google review page pops open instantly.)

You: "That took 1 second flat.
Right now, you serve 400 people a day. Your happiest customers leave here planning to give you 5 stars, but by the time they get to their car, they forget your exact spelling, get distracted, and that review is gone forever.
With this smart stand sitting right next to your Moneris terminal, your staff doesn't have to feel awkward. When a guest says 'The meal was amazing', your staff just says: 'Awesome, give this a quick tap with your phone.'
I have two pre-programmed units in my car right now. I can leave them on your counter for 14 days for free. If you don't collect at least 15 reviews by next Friday, I pick them up and it costs you zero. Fair enough?"
```

### Key Objections in Tier 2:
- **"My staff will forget to ask."**
  - *Response*: *"That’s why we provide our laminated '7-Word Ask' sticker that goes right behind the register. They never ask 'Can you leave a review?' — they just ask: 'Did you love everything today?' When the customer smiles, they point to the card. It becomes muscle memory in 24 hours."*
- **"We already have a QR code on the menu."**
  - *Response*: *"QR codes are okay, but in Saskatoon, 85% of people pay with Apple Pay or Google Pay. Tapping is second nature. NFC has zero friction. Let's place this next to your QR code for 7 days and see which one collects 5x more reviews."*
